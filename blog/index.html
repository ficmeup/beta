The Best Way to Listen to Fanfic: Fic Me Up vs. Every Text-to-Speech App

If you want to listen to AO3 fanfic, Fic Me Up is the one tool built for exactly that, and it costs nothing. General text-to-speech apps can read a fic too, but they were made for PDFs, articles and textbooks, and it shows.

Open Fic Me Up

Why listen to fic at all?

Listening lets you keep a 300k slow burn going while you cook, commute, clean or try to fall asleep. Most of us have tried to make that work with whatever voice app was on hand. The usual results: the app reads out the rating, the warnings and all 40 tags before the story starts. It loses your place between chapters. Or the good voices sit behind a $140-a-year paywall.

This post looks at the free and paid text-to-speech options people actually use for fic, what each one costs, and where Fic Me Up does better for AO3 readers. Where another app is the better pick, we say so.

The free options

The free tools will read a fic, but none of them know what a fic is. They read whatever is on the page, tags and all, and keep no library of your works.

  • Safari's Listen to Page and Speak Screen (iPhone, iPad, Mac). Built in and free. Open the work in Reader view, then tap Listen to Page for speed controls and sentence skips. It uses Apple's system voices and only works on pages that support Reader. On a chaptered fic you have to load Entire Work first or it stops at each chapter.
  • Microsoft Edge Read Aloud (Windows, Mac, iOS, Android). Read Aloud has some of the best free voices around, with natural-sounding accents. Offline you only get a few voices. It reads the page as it is, so you pick the starting paragraph yourself and lose your place when you close the tab.
  • Read Aloud extension (Chrome, Firefox, Edge). Read Aloud is open source and uses your browser's voices, plus cloud voices from Google, Amazon, IBM and Microsoft. Some cloud voices need an in-app purchase. It's a desktop tool, not a phone one.
  • @Voice Aloud Reader (Android). @Voice is free with ads, or $15 once to remove them. It opens AO3's EPUB and PDF downloads and has a sleep timer, so it's a long-time favourite with Android fic readers. Voice quality depends on the voices installed on your phone.
  • Free tiers of paid apps. Speechify's free plan gives you 10 robotic-sounding voices and a 1.5x speed cap. ElevenReader's free plan gives you 10 hours a month. NaturalReader's free tier allows 20 minutes a day of its Premium voices and 5 minutes of its Plus voices. These are trials, not a way to get through a 200k fic.

The paid options

Paying gets you better voices, not a better way to read fic. All four apps below are made for documents, study and audiobooks, and the best voices in most of them run on the company's servers. Prices are in the table in the next section.

  • Speechify. The best-known name. Premium has 1,000+ voices in 60+ languages, speeds up to 5x, and extras like scanning and AI summaries. It's the most expensive option here, and much of what you pay for is study and work features a fic reader won't use.
  • ElevenReader. Made by ElevenLabs, whose AI voices are among the most human-sounding you can get. The free 10 hours a month goes quickly on a long fic. The paid Ultra plan has a fair-use cap of 24 hours of audio a day. You need an account and an internet connection, because the voices are made on ElevenLabs' servers.
  • NaturalReader. Three paid plans: Lite, Plus and Pro. Lite gives unlimited listening with its basic voices. The better AI voices need Plus or Pro, which allow 500,000 characters of listening a day.
  • Voice Dream Reader. A long-time favourite for EPUBs on Apple devices. It opens DRM-free EPUB, PDF, Word and more, and you can buy extra premium voices. It now needs a subscription to use at all.

Why Fic Me Up is better for fic

Fic Me Up was built around how AO3 works and how fic readers listen. It runs in the browser on iPhone, iPad, Android and computers. Add it to your Home Screen and it works like an app, with no app store.

  • It understands an AO3 work. The rating, warnings, tags and stats show on screen but aren't read aloud, so the voice starts with the story. Press play once and it reads to the end, chapter to chapter. The chapter list stays at the top so you can jump anywhere.
  • Natural voices, free and unlimited. Twelve Piper voices work on phones and computers, and Linda and Cori are trained on audiobook narration. Computers also get twelve Kokoro voices. There are no hour caps, character caps or speed paywalls, and speed runs from 0.5x to 2x.
  • Your fics stay on your device. There's no account and no sign-up. The voices run on your own phone or computer, so nothing is uploaded. Each voice downloads once, about 60 MB for a Piper voice.
  • It never touches AO3's servers. Fic Me Up makes no requests to AO3. You download the work as an EPUB, paste the text, or use the Listen bookmark on a page you already have open.
  • It keeps reading with the screen locked. With a Piper voice, play, pause and skip work from the lock screen, iPhone included.
  • It's a library, not a one-off reader. Every fic remembers its place to the sentence, and you can tap any sentence to start there. A queue starts the next fic 8 seconds after one ends. You also get a sleep timer, notes on each fic, and playlists you can share as AO3 links.
  • It sends you back to the writer. There are no ratings inside the app. When a work ends, Fic Me Up shows a button to leave kudos on AO3, because kudos and comments are what writers actually see.

It reads work and school documents too

Fic Me Up isn't only for fic. Open a PDF, a Word file (.docx), an EPUB, an HTML page or a text file, or paste in any text, and it reads it aloud the same way. That makes it handy for reports, readings, articles and study notes, with the same free voices, speed control, sleep timer and saved place. There's also a Work room, a plain white look with no AO3 wording, for when you're using it on documents.

It can't open books bought through Apple Books or Kindle, because those are copy-protected.

Where other apps still win

Fic Me Up is a small, free project made by one fan, and some things are still better elsewhere.

  • Voice range. ElevenReader's voices sound more human, and Speechify has far more voices and languages. Fic Me Up has 24 voices, and the 12 Kokoro voices only work on computers.
  • Syncing between devices. A fic added on your phone isn't on your computer yet. On iPhone, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate libraries. Optional sign-in with an email code is in the works.
  • Older phones. The voices run on your phone's own processor, so older phones can struggle and drain the battery faster. A warm or low-battery phone slows the voice down too.
  • The first download. Each Piper voice is about 60 MB, so use Wi-Fi the first time.
  • Your iPhone's premium voices. Safari only lets websites use Apple's basic built-in voices. The Premium, Enhanced and Siri voices you've downloaded in Settings won't appear.

If you mostly listen on an Android phone and already love @Voice, it's a fine free choice for EPUBs. If voice quality matters more than anything and you don't mind paying, ElevenReader sounds the most human.

The verdict

For listening to AO3 fanfic, Fic Me Up is the best pick. It's free with no limits, it starts with the story instead of the tags, it keeps your place in every fic, and your library never leaves your device.

Try it in two minutes:

  1. Open Fic Me Up on your phone or computer. On iPhone, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  2. On AO3, open a work, tap Entire Work, then Download and EPUB.
  3. In Fic Me Up, tap Open a file, pick the fic, choose a voice and press play.

The guide covers the other ways to add a fic, including the Listen bookmark and paste.

One last thing: the writer gets nothing from your listening. When you finish a fic, go back to AO3 and leave kudos or a comment.
